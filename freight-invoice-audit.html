What you receive per packet

A packet is one freight invoice with its supporting transport documents, not one PDF file.

  • extraction.json — one record per document, field level, with page references
  • extraction.csv — the same rows for the accounting import
  • exceptions.md — every item that needs a human, with the reason
  • reconciliation.md — the arithmetic and the derived measures (cost per pound, surcharge share, declared-value ratio)
  • One audit note stating which checks ran and which did not

The review, in order

  1. Extraction. Scanned and image documents are read by OCR; text-based PDFs are read by text extraction. An image-only PDF is never assumed to contain text.
  2. Confidence marking. Every extracted field carries a confidence marker. A field that cannot be read goes to a human-review list; it is not guessed.
  3. Arithmetic checks. Line items are summed and compared with the invoice total; accessorial and surcharge subtotals are compared with the line items they are claimed to cover; a fuel surcharge percentage is compared with the carrier’s published index for the invoice date.
  4. Duplicate checks. Invoice numbers, and bill of lading, PRO and tracking numbers, are normalised and compared across the reviewed set.
  5. Contract-rate checks. Each charged amount is compared with the contracted rate or the carrier’s published tariff.
  6. Triaging. Findings are separated into three amounts that are never combined: potential overcharge, verified billing discrepancy, and confirmed recovery. They are never summed into a single “saved” figure.

Data handling and scope

Data handling. Documents are processed for the run and returned as output files. Extracted text and outputs are written to your own folder, not a shared pool. No document content is used to train a model. Retention is agreed per engagement, and deletion is a written instruction, not a setting someone has to remember.
Scope. This is a document review and reconciliation service. It is not an independent financial audit and not legal advice, and it expresses no audit opinion. No recovery is guaranteed: any dispute depends on the carrier’s own dispute rules and on documentary evidence that must still be assembled. Industry figures quoted on the worked-example page are those publishers’ statements with their source and read date, not measurements of any client’s freight bills.
